The approach / 01—04

From Question to Deliverable

Most projects started with a specific question. I defined what needed to be understood, gathered the relevant evidence, structured it for comparison, and turned the result into something the team could use.

  1. 01

    Define the Question

    Clarify what the project actually needs to know.

  2. 02

    Find the Evidence

    Collect relevant information from reports, public databases, company disclosures, APIs, or other appropriate sources.

  3. 03

    Structure & Check

    Organize information into comparable fields and verify missing, unusual, or conflicting results.

  4. 04

    Make It Usable

    Turn the findings into a proposal, analysis, dataset, visual output, or discussion material.

Delta Electronics / Research quality

Three Problems I Had to Fix in the Research Workflow

Problems that only showed up once the research ran at scale, and what I changed.

01 — Inconsistent source formats

PDF formats varied widely across company reports. In some cases, the primary extraction method returned an empty page even when readable text was present.

I added a fallback extraction step before allowing the workflow to classify the information as missing.

Page level11.15%

20,735 of 185,893 pages recovered through fallback extraction

88.85% primary extraction11.15% fallback recovered
Company level65.2%

1,170 of 1,795 companies required fallback on at least one page

02 — A failed query is not a “no data” result

During one run, a connection issue affected 615 queries across 100 companies. The requests had not successfully reached the source, but the output initially looked the same as a valid “no data found” result.

I changed the logic so failed requests were flagged for review instead of being stored as research results.

Before the fix, a failed request and a genuine “no data” result were stored the same way.

03 — Automated classification still needed review

Automation provided a useful first-pass classification, but difficult cases still required checking against the original disclosure.

In a deep review of 106 companies, 16 classifications were corrected or refined, and recurring error patterns were used to improve later runs.
